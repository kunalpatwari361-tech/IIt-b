Nirnay: a humanless underwriting engine for Home Loans and LAP

Nirnay (निर्णय, “decision”) takes a Home Loan or Loan Against Property application from the first tap to an Approve, Refer or Reject decision. Clean cases need no person. Every check runs as code, every rule points to the regulation it comes from, and an auditor can replay every decision.

The short version

Problem Statement 2 asks for an engine that underwrites Home Loans and LAP with no person in the decision path. It has to follow NHB/RBI rules, internal credit policy, KYC/AML law, the Fair Practices Code and audit standards. Nirnay is our answer.

  • Customers apply on a mobile app or the web portal. Consent screens let the engine pull data from credit bureaus, Account Aggregators, UIDAI, GSTN, EPFO, state land records and CERSAI.
  • The engine runs eight automated gates: identity, AML, fraud and documents, policy rules, property, affordability, risk grade and authority limit. It returns Approve, Refer or Reject with reason codes.
  • A compliance gate blocks any decision that breaks a coded regulation. Every decision is saved as a tamper-evident decision record that can be replayed years later.

Six choices that make it work

Two-stage decision

An in-principle offer in minutes from the customer's own data, and a final sanction once property checks finish. Builder projects we have already checked skip most property work.

Rules and AI kept apart

Rules are versioned tables the credit team edits. Models produce scores. A large language model (LLM) reads documents and drafts text, and never sets an outcome.

Compliance as code

Each regulation becomes a rule tagged with its source clause. If a rule fails, no decision goes out.

Refer is a feature

Cases the rules say need a person, such as a politically exposed customer, go to an officer with a ready file. Officer decisions train the next model.

Replay any decision

The same inputs with the same rule and model versions always give the same answer, so audits are exact.

One engine, many front doors

App, portal, partner app and branch staff call the same APIs, so the same rules apply everywhere.

The problem in plain words

Underwriting is the lender's answer to three questions. Can this person repay? Will they repay? Is the property good security if they don't? For a Home Loan the property is being bought. For LAP the customer already owns it and borrows against it.

Today a file moves through many hands. Sales collects papers. Operations checks KYC. Credit reads salary slips and bank statements. A lawyer checks the title. A valuer visits the property. Each team re-checks what the last one did, files go back to the customer for missing papers, and the customer waits days.

Problem Statement 2 asks us to replace that chain with one engine that decides on its own, follows every rule, and can show why it decided.

What “humanless” means in our design

  • No person is needed to approve or reject a clean file.
  • People still own the rules, the models and the hard cases. The engine sends a case to a person (Refer) when a regulation requires it, for example a politically exposed person, or when data is missing or uncertain.
  • A customer can always ask for a person to review a rejection through the grievance process.

Why this is hard

  • Data is scattered. Bureaus, banks, income tax, GST, PF and land records in every state all hold a piece.
  • Property is the slowest part. Land records and encumbrance certificates are online in many states, not all, and formats differ.
  • Self-employed income is hard to read. Many LAP customers have real income that is poorly documented.
  • Fraud targets every step. Fake salary slips, edited bank statements, one property mortgaged twice, agents and valuers working together.
  • Rules change. RBI consolidated its directions on 28 November 2025 and keeps amending them.
  • Every decision needs an explanation that the customer, the auditor and the supervisor can each follow.

A note on “NHB regulations”. Since 9 August 2019 the RBI regulates housing finance companies, and the National Housing Bank (NHB) supervises them through inspections and regulatory returns. Most rules that used to be NHB directions now sit in RBI's Housing Finance Companies Directions, consolidated again on 28 November 2025. In this report, “NHB/RBI rules” means both.

One engine, two front doors: app and web portal

Customers, partners and staff all reach the same engine through the same APIs. The mobile app is for doing things on the move with a camera. The web portal is for longer work at a desk, and it is where staff, vendors and auditors work.

Mobile app (Android and iOS)

Customer app

  • Sign in with mobile OTP; the device is bound to the account.
  • Auto-fill from PAN, Aadhaar eKYC or DigiLocker, so the customer types very little.
  • Plain-language consent screens for the bureau pull, Account Aggregator and the DPDP privacy notice.
  • Camera: selfie with liveness, document capture with blur and glare checks, geo-tagged property photos and a short video walk-through.
  • Quick eligibility and EMI options before the full application.
  • Decision screen with reasons in English, Hindi and other Indian languages.
  • Key Facts Statement, eSign, and eNACH or UPI AutoPay inside the app.
  • Live status, WhatsApp and SMS updates, a help assistant, and one-tap grievance with officer details.

Partner app (DSA, connector, builder sales)

  • Start a lead; the customer gives consent on their own phone, so the partner never sees OTPs.
  • See pending items and payouts per file.
  • Builder sales can look up units in pre-approved projects.

Web portal

  • Customer portal: the same journey as the app on a laptop; invite a co-applicant; download letters.
  • Partner portal: bulk leads; builders upload RERA papers, approvals and plans once to get a project pre-approved.
  • Credit console: the Refer queue with an AI-drafted file note, evidence viewer, re-pull of data, a decision with a mandatory reason, SLA timers, and a second approver for large loans.
  • Vendor portal: empanelled lawyers and valuers receive referred cases, upload reports, and see SLAs.
  • Compliance and audit portal: search and replay any decision, see rule and model versions, export evidence, regulatory reports, Fair Practices metrics such as turnaround times, rejection reasons and complaints.
  • Policy studio: edit LTV, FOIR and score cut-offs as tables; maker-checker approval; test on past cases before going live; effective dates.
  • Model governance console: model inventory, drift and fairness dashboards, override rates, and a kill switch that sends all new cases to Refer.

End-to-end flow: application to decision

The flow has two stages. Stage A uses data about the person and ends in an in-principle offer. Stage B adds the property and ends in the final sanction. Both stages end in the same three outcomes.

The engine itself decides in under two seconds once its inputs are in. Time in the flow goes to waiting for data: the customer filling the form, the Account Aggregator returning statements, a state portal returning an encumbrance certificate. If a source is slow, the application waits in a clear status the customer can see. The engine never guesses missing data.

Pre-approved projects. A builder's project is checked once for title, approvals and RERA registration, then marked pre-approved. A unit in that project only needs unit-level checks, so Stage B can finish in minutes and many Home Loans complete in one sitting.

Solution architecture (SA)

Seven layers. Requests come in through the channels and pass the gateway. The orchestrator runs each application as a state machine and calls the decision services in order. Services get outside data through the integration hub and write evidence to the data layer. Every step emits an event that the audit ledger, notifications and dashboards consume.

What each layer does

Channels
Customer and partner apps, and the web portals for customers, partners, credit officers, auditors and policy owners. All are thin clients over the same APIs.
Access and security
API gateway with a web application firewall, OAuth2/OpenID Connect login, role-based access, rate limits, device binding, and masking of personal data in logs.
Orchestration
A durable workflow engine keeps one state machine per application: which steps ran, what they returned, what is pending, and SLA timers. Steps are idempotent, so a retried bureau call does not create a second enquiry on the customer's report.
Decision services
Nine services with one job each. AI-backed ones return scores and facts; rules-based ones apply policy and regulation. The decision service combines their results into one outcome.
Data and integration
The integration hub talks to outside sources. The application database holds state, the feature store serves model inputs, the vault keeps documents, and the append-only audit ledger keeps decision records.
Core systems
The loan origination and management systems, CRM, eSign and e-stamp, eNACH and UPI AutoPay mandates, and notifications receive events after sanction.
Platform
India-region hosting, Kubernetes, encryption with managed keys, a security monitoring (SIEM) feed, full observability, and a disaster-recovery site.

AI solution architecture (AI SA)

AI does three jobs in Nirnay. It reads documents, photos and deeds. It predicts income, default risk, fraud and property value. It writes file notes and help answers. It does not decide. The decision engine sets every outcome from rules and approved models, after each AI output has passed the guardrails.

Six rules we follow for AI

  1. Outcomes come from rules and validated models. An LLM never decides.
  2. Every model has an owner, a model card, a version and a validation report before it goes live.
  3. No protected attributes as inputs (gender, religion, caste, marital status). We also test for proxies such as PIN code.
  4. Every AI output carries a confidence. Below the threshold, the case goes to Refer.
  5. Models and data stay in India-region infrastructure. Personal data is masked in LLM prompts, and providers may not train on our data.
  6. A kill switch sends every new case to Refer if monitoring shows a problem.

Model lifecycle (MLOps)

  1. Data: versioned training sets built from the feature store, so any model can be rebuilt exactly.
  2. Train: scorecard and challenger trained on the same data split.
  3. Validate: an independent reviewer checks accuracy (Gini, KS), stability, fairness and explanations.
  4. Approve: the model risk committee signs off; the model card goes to the registry.
  5. Shadow: the model scores live cases without affecting decisions; we compare it with the live model.
  6. Canary: a small share of traffic uses the new model, with automatic rollback.
  7. Live and monitored: drift (population stability index above 0.25 triggers review), performance on matured loans, approval and refer rates by segment, override rates, latency.
  8. Retrain: on a schedule or when a monitor fires, back to step 1.

Cold start. We do not have Hero's past loan data in the hackathon. Our demo model trains on the public Home Credit Default Risk dataset and we will say so in the demo. In production the scorecard is built on Hero's own loan book. Until there is enough history, it runs on bureau score plus an expert scorecard.

After the decision, the same hub connects to execution services: Aadhaar eSign, e-stamping, eNACH and UPI AutoPay mandates, bank account verification (penny drop), SMS and WhatsApp, and the loan systems.

How the hub keeps data reliable

  • Consent first. No call goes out without a valid consent for that purpose; consents are stored with their artefact IDs.
  • One adapter per source. Each adapter turns the source's format into our standard format and has its own timeout and retry rules.
  • Fallbacks. If the Account Aggregator is down, the customer can upload a PDF statement and Document AI reads it. The engine marks which path was used.
  • Circuit breakers. A failing source is paused so it does not slow every application; affected steps wait in a clear status.
  • Evidence kept. The raw response is stored with a SHA-256 hash, and the decision record points to it.
  • Quality checks. Names, dates and amounts are normalised, and records from different sources are matched on PAN, mobile and date of birth.

2Requirement 2 of 8

Eligibility and policy

Asked: assess eligibility in real time for Home Loans and LAP, and validate each case against internal credit policies.

Credit policy lives in the engine as decision tables, not hard-wired code. The credit team edits them in the policy studio. Every change needs a second approver (maker-checker), carries a version and an effective date, and is tested against past applications before it goes live. Home Loans and LAP have separate rule sets.

How the eligible amount is worked out

Max EMI = net monthly income × FOIR cap − existing EMIs
Loan by income = Max EMI × [1 − (1 + r)−n] ÷ r (r = monthly rate, n = months)
Loan by property = LTV cap × property value
Eligible loan = the lowest of: loan by income, loan by property, product limit, amount asked

Worked example: Ravi, 32, salaried, buying a ₹60 lakh flat

  • Net income ₹1,00,000 a month; existing EMI ₹10,000; asks ₹45 lakh for 20 years at 8.75%.
  • FOIR cap 50% → max EMI = ₹50,000 − ₹10,000 = ₹40,000.
  • At 8.75% for 240 months, each ₹1 of EMI supports about ₹113.16 of loan → loan by income ≈ ₹45.26 lakh.
  • A loan between ₹30 lakh and ₹75 lakh has an 80% LTV cap → loan by property = ₹48 lakh.
  • Eligible = lowest of ₹45.26 lakh, ₹48 lakh and the ₹45 lakh asked → ₹45 lakh, EMI ₹39,767. Total EMIs are 49.8% of income and LTV is 75%.

Product-specific checks

Home Loan

  • Source of the customer's own contribution traced in bank data.
  • Builder and project in the pre-approved list, or RERA-registered with a clean record.
  • Disbursal schedule tied to construction stage for under-construction homes.
  • Stamp duty and registration are left out of the property cost for LTV, except where RBI allows them for homes costing ₹10 lakh or less.

LAP

  • End-use declaration captured; end-use monitoring set up after disbursal.
  • Property owned by the applicant or co-applicant; no undisclosed charge.
  • Business vintage and income from GST and ITR agree with bank credits.
  • Existing loan on the property must be taken over or closed at disbursal.

Real time. All rules run in under a second once features are ready. A quick check runs on declared data before the full application, so the customer sees a realistic amount early.

3Requirement 3 of 8

Fraud detection and KYC/AML

Asked: detect fraud and meet KYC/AML requirements without manual review.

KYC, fully digital

  1. Mobile OTP and device binding. The device fingerprint is saved for fraud checks.
  2. PAN check. Name and status from the PAN service, plus the Aadhaar–PAN link status.
  3. Aadhaar. eKYC through a licensed partner where Hero is permitted, or Aadhaar offline XML or DigiLocker. Aadhaar numbers are stored only as tokens in an Aadhaar Data Vault, as UIDAI requires.
  4. CKYCR. Search and download an existing record; upload new records after onboarding.
  5. Face match and liveness. The selfie is matched to the ID photo; passive liveness rejects photos of photos and masks.
  6. Fallback. If the face match fails, the customer is offered video KYC (V-CIP) with an official, as RBI's KYC Directions allow.

AML, without manual review for clear cases

  • Screening: names and dates of birth are checked against the UN Security Council lists, the lists under the UAPA, PEP lists and adverse media, at onboarding and again whenever a list updates.
  • Smart matching: fuzzy, phonetic and cross-script matching handles Indian name spellings, so “Mohammed” and “Muhammad” or “Choudhary” and “Chaudhari” are compared properly.
  • Auto-clearing: a name hit is cleared automatically when date of birth, PAN, father's name or nationality clearly differ. Only real possible matches reach a person.
  • Risk category: each customer is rated low, medium or high from occupation, geography, product and source of funds. High risk gets enhanced checks.
  • Rules that need a person: a politically exposed person needs enhanced checks and senior management approval under the KYC Directions, so the engine refers these cases by design.
  • No tipping-off: the customer never sees an AML reason. Their message says only that the application needs more review. Suspicious cases are prepared for the Principal Officer, who files with FIU-IND.

4Requirement 4 of 8

Risk scoring

Asked: score the risk of every application and show how the score is produced.

How the score is produced

  1. Features: the feature store gives about 40 inputs in four groups: repayment history, affordability, stability and collateral.
  2. Points: the scorecard gives each input points. For Ravi: base 650, repayment history +33, no missed payments +10, affordability +10, collateral +15, salaried stability +20.
  3. Total and calibration: points add up to 738. Calibration on past loans maps each score to a probability of default.
  4. Grade: 738 falls in grade B. The grade sets the decision band and the price spread under Hero's board-approved interest-rate policy.
  5. Reasons: the factors that lost the most points become the reason codes. In the challenger, SHAP values play the same role.

5Requirement 5 of 8

Documents and property

Asked: verify documents digitally and assess the property within the same flow.

Document AI pipeline

  1. Capture: in-app camera with edge detection, or PDF upload; DigiLocker documents come in already signed by the issuer.
  2. Quality check: blur, glare and cropping are caught on the phone before upload.
  3. Classify: the model tells a salary slip from a Form 16, a sale deed from an allotment letter.
  4. Extract: fields such as name, PAN, employer, gross and net pay, survey number and owner are read, each with a confidence.
  5. Verify: each field is checked against a source, as in the table below.
  6. Tamper check: file metadata, font consistency, pixel patterns and QR codes are checked for edits.
  7. Store: the file goes to the encrypted vault with a SHA-256 hash that the decision record references.

Property assessment in the same flow

Identify
Address, survey or CTS number and map pin; for flats, the RERA project ID, tower and unit. If the project is pre-approved, most checks below are already done.
Ownership
The owner in state land records must match the seller (HL) or the applicant (LAP). Mutation status is checked.
Encumbrance
Encumbrance certificate from the state registration department, CERSAI search for an existing mortgage, and e-Courts search for cases.
Legal reading
The LLM deed reader pulls parties, survey numbers, dates and each transfer in the ownership chain, in English or regional languages. Every fact must point to text in the deed. Gaps or conflicts become flags.
Valuation
The AVM gives a value range from registered prices, circle rates, listings, building age and location. The circle rate acts as a floor check. If the declared price is far above the AVM, the case is flagged.
Inspection
For low-value or pre-approved cases, the customer records geo-tagged, time-stamped photos and a short video in the app. Otherwise an empanelled valuer does it through the vendor app. Photos are checked against satellite images and for reuse.
Technical
Approved plan, occupancy or completion certificate, building age, and flood or hazard zone from public maps.
Output
Market value, the value used for LTV (the lower of AVM and valuer value), and a legal status: clear needs review defective.

Where this stops being instant. Land records are digitised unevenly across states. Where a record is not machine-readable, the engine refers the title to an empanelled lawyer through the vendor portal. The rest of the file stays digital and the SLA is tracked.

Audit standards built in

  • Maker-checker on every rule, model and template change, with both names stored.
  • Tamper-evident trail: each decision record includes the hash of the one before it, so a changed or deleted record breaks the chain. Records are kept on write-once storage.
  • Read-only audit portal for internal audit, statutory auditors and NHB inspection teams, with replay of any decision.
  • Risk-based internal audit: the audit portal gives the sample selection and exception reports that RBI's risk-based internal audit rules expect of larger HFCs.
  • Regulatory returns are generated from the same data, so reported numbers match the engine.
  • Regulation changes are handled in the policy studio: update the rule, cite the new clause, test on past cases, approve, then switch on at the effective date.

What each outcome does

Approve

In-principle or final sanction letter with the KFS, the rate and EMI options, then eSign and the repayment mandate. A counter-offer is an approval for a lower amount or a different tenure, with the reason shown.

Refer

The case lands in the credit console with a file note, evidence links and the engine's draft offer. The officer must record a reason. Their decision is saved to the decision record and becomes a training label.

Reject

A written letter in the customer's language with the reasons (AML reasons excepted), what they can do next, such as adding a co-applicant, and how to ask for a review or raise a grievance.

Explainability and governance

Asked: every decision must be explainable, traceable and audit-ready.

Three audiences, three views of the same decision

Customer

Plain reasons in their language, what they can do next, and how to appeal. AML reasons are never shown.

Credit officer and auditor

Every gate's result, the rule that fired with its source clause, the points per factor, and links to the raw evidence.

Supervisor and model validator

Replay of any decision with the original inputs and the rule and model versions of that day, plus model cards and validation reports.

Reason codes

Every refer or reject reason has a code. Each code has an internal meaning and an approved customer message in each language.

The decision record

Every decision writes one record to the audit ledger. It holds the inputs (or their evidence hashes), the version of every rule set and model used, each gate's result, the score with its points, the reason codes, the outcome, any officer action, and the hash of the previous record. Feeding the same inputs into the same versions gives the same output, so any decision can be replayed exactly. The simulator below builds a sample record live.

Governance

  • Board-approved policies for credit, model risk and AI, with named owners.
  • AI and model risk committee that approves every model and every material rule change.
  • Model inventory with a model card for each model: purpose, data, limits, performance, fairness results, owner.
  • Independent validation before go-live and at least once a year.
  • Monitoring: drift, performance on matured loans, approval and refer rates by segment, and how often officers overturn the engine.
  • Fairness: no protected attributes; proxy tests; approval-rate gaps reviewed by the committee.
  • Kill switch: one action routes every new case to Refer.
